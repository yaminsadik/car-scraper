Used-Car Search Workflow

How a Marketplace run actually works, stage by stage — and why each gate is where it is. Section numbers refer to used_car_buying_playbook.md. Rebuilt 2026-08-20 after the brand-loop model was retired.

Two ideas run this whole design: rejection should be free, and don't scroll the same feed twice.

Opening a listing is the expensive step. Every rejection that happens before that — from the card, or mechanically during capture — costs nothing. Separately: Facebook's own filters can't narrow the feed by brand, so the sweep no longer tries. It varies price and sort only — the two parameters that actually change what comes back — and lets a tool, not a human judgment call, do the repetitive extraction and dedup.

The 16-checkpoint grid

4 price slices × 4 sorts. No brand column — Facebook's Make filter doesn't restrict or resample the feed (make=Honda and make=Toyota returned 20 of the same 24 listings, tested live 2026-08-20). Brand separation happens later, during triage. The fourth sort, Suggested, is Facebook's default sort and is represented by omitting sortBy from the URL.

Stage by stage

Stage 0

Load state

Read the playbook, the checkpoint grid, and the tracker before touching a browser.

Three files, three questions: what are the rules, which grid cell is next, and which listings have already been judged. The grid cell — not a brand — is what "resume" means now.
↓
Stage 1

Build the URL for one grid cell

One price slice, one sort. No brand parameter.

Only price and sort are real. minPrice/maxPrice are applied strictly. sortBy genuinely returns a different slice of the pool per value. make=, topLevelVehicleType=, maxYear= and carType= are all cosmetic — see the callout below — so none of them are worth setting.
↓
Stage 2

Human scroll

You scroll the tab to the bottom by hand. The assistant does not scroll.

Automated scrolling never fires Facebook's loader — confirmed at the network layer, zero fetch requests on programmatic scroll. Manual scrolling with ordinary pauses works normally and has produced feeds over 300 cards deep from a single query.
↓
Stage 3

mp.js read --out

Extracts every card from the DOM — id, year, make, model, price, city, true SERP rank — to one JSON file per pass.

Mechanical, zero judgment. Reads the tracking blob Facebook attaches to each result link for its real search-result position, which is more reliable than DOM order alone. One file per capture, never hand-edited.
↓
Stage 4

merge.js

Folds every capture JSON into one deduped captures/master.csv. Drops motorcycles/boats/trailers by keyword; nothing else.

Also mechanical — no brand list, no price ceiling, no criteria live in this file (see CLAUDE.md: tools move cards, the playbook decides). It tracks which passes saw each listing and flags price changes between passes, which is what makes 16 overlapping scrolls cheap instead of repetitive.
↓
Stage 5 — the important one

Card-level triage

Read master.csv. Six checks, all free — nothing opened yet.

Title triage is necessary but not sufficient. A real capture caught a Hyundai Sonata typed as "Honda Sonata" by the seller — brand-matching on title text alone produces false positives, so survivors still get a second look before scoring. Minivans and cargo vans have also slipped through a brand match on this same pass; body type needs its own check even for an approved brand.
↓
Stage 6

Dedupe against the tracker

Permanent rejections never re-open. Price rejections do.

Because the grid runs the same pool through up to 16 overlapping passes (measured overlap on one pair of passes: 83%), this step is where most of the time savings actually come from — not from finding new cars, but from instantly skipping ones already judged.
↓
Stage 7

Open the listing

One reusable tab. Mileage and title status live only here, never on the card.

The full description is also only here — financing language, disclosed defects, and curbstoning tells are read once and captured completely, never reopened for one more field.
↓
Stage 8

Hard filters, reliability, scoring

Mileage · title · red flags (§6) · exact model/powertrain reliability · 8+ Strong · 4–7 Worth a look · below 4 Marginal.

Unverified financing-ad prices score zero on price band and are capped below Strong until the seller confirms a real cash price. Reliability research is the most expensive step in the pipeline, which is why it only ever runs on listings that survived everything free first.
↓
Stage 9

Persist & checkpoint

Update the tracker and checkpoint every 5 listings, then run the read-only validate.js before handoff.

Two files, two jobs — master.csv is disposable and rebuildable from the raw JSONs; seen_listings.md is the curated, hand-meaningful record and is never rewritten from scratch.

Final stage

Verification — you, not the assistant

The assistant never contacts sellers. It writes the message; you send it.

Bands are computed from Pembroke Pines now, not Fort Lauderdale (§7) — Miami-Dade got closer, Boca Raton and Palm Beach County got further. The bar scales with the drive, not with how promising the listing looks.

The three-tier log

Tier 1 — raw capture

captures/feeds/*.json → captures/master.csv

  • Every vehicle card, every brand, every price
  • Written by mp.js + merge.js, mechanically
  • Never edited by hand
  • Disposable — master.csv regenerates from the JSONs anytime

Tier 2 — curated tracker

seen_listings.md

  • Only approved-brand cars actually triaged
  • Written by the assistant, with reasoning
  • Hand-meaningful — this is what you'd actually read
  • Append/update in place, never rewritten from scratch

Tier 3 — deal tracker

deals/<listing-id>.md → deals/INDEX.md

  • Only the handful that turned into a conversation
  • What the seller said, what the VIN showed, what the photos showed, when you're meeting
  • One small file per deal — edit it without touching the big tracker
  • node mp.js deal due tells you what §11 still owes before you drive

The thing Facebook doesn't tell you

Almost every vehicle filter in the sidebar is cosmetic. Make, Body style, Vehicle type (Cars & Trucks), and Year all accept the parameter, render as "active" in the sidebar, and change nothing about the returned results. Year was the last one tested: with maxYear=2018 showing active, the feed still returned a 2022, a 2023, a 2025 and a 2026 model year. Make doesn't even resample — make=Honda and make=Toyota returned 20 of the same 24 IDs at identical price/sort/location. The radius parameter is cosmetic too; the real 40-mile radius is a soft relevance hint, not a boundary — out-of-radius cities appear interleaved throughout the feed, not appended after nearby ones run out.

What actually works: minPrice/maxPrice (strict), sortBy (genuinely resamples), and maxMileage (advisory — leaks, always re-verify on the listing page). That's the entire filter surface the grid is built on. Everything else is triaged by hand, from the card — that's the whole reason Stage 5 exists.
